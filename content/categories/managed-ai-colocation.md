Managed AI colocation is the part of the ecosystem where the buyer is sourcing deployed accelerator capacity, managed GPU clusters, or AI-ready operating environments instead of building the entire stack from land, shell, and electrical systems upward.

That makes this branch launch-critical. Serious buyers do not only compare modular builds, direct ownership, and wholesale colocation. They also compare whether a managed operator can deliver time-to-compute, faster onboarding, and lower execution risk for a given training or inference program.

## What belongs here

- GPU cloud and managed cluster operators
- AI-ready colocation providers with meaningful hosting and operations layers
- platforms that package deployed accelerator capacity, infrastructure support, and enterprise onboarding

## What this category is adjacent to

- **AI Colocation / GPU Hosting** because managed AI colocation is a higher-service operating layer within the broader capacity market
- **High-Density Rack Power** because serious managed GPU environments still depend on dense electrical design and resilient power delivery
- **Liquid Cooling** because hosted AI capacity increasingly depends on high-density thermal architecture, not generic white-space assumptions
- **Network Fabric / Connectivity** because model training, distributed inference, and cluster-to-cluster traffic make interconnect quality a core buying variable
- **Site / Land / Permitting** because many managed providers compete on access to already-developed, power-ready environments

## Executive questions this branch helps answer

- Is the buyer choosing between self-build and already-operated AI capacity?
- Which operators are credible for enterprise-grade hosted GPU environments?
- Where does managed AI hosting become a substitute for dedicated colocation procurement?
- Which adjacent infrastructure layers still determine whether a managed operator is truly credible?

## Current research posture

This category should feel like an institutional map of the managed AI capacity market, not a thin cloud list. The important distinction is whether the company meaningfully participates in AI infrastructure delivery, operations, and enterprise-grade capacity access.
