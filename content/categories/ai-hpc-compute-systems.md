# AI & HPC Compute Systems

AI and HPC compute systems are the hardware and integrated platform layer that turns adjacent power, cooling, networking, and facility readiness into actual usable cluster capacity. This category exists for buyers evaluating the compute system itself rather than only the hosting environment around it.

It covers direct compute-system suppliers: server OEMs, integrated AI platform vendors, composable compute infrastructure providers, and clustered system vendors whose product is meaningfully the accelerator-dense compute platform.

## What belongs here

- GPU server OEMs and accelerator-dense system vendors
- integrated AI and HPC platform suppliers
- clustered compute-system providers
- composable compute infrastructure vendors
- direct system-level compute platforms for AI training, inference, and HPC

## What does not belong here

- generic colocation or GPU hosting unless the company is also directly supplying the compute system as a product
- power, cooling, or networking vendors whose role is adjacent but not compute-system primary
- software-only observability or orchestration vendors

## Why buyers separate this market

- compute-system choices determine density, serviceability, topology, and deployment envelope
- server and cluster architecture affect cooling design, power distribution, and interconnect assumptions
- buyers comparing hosted capacity versus owned or semi-owned infrastructure need to identify who actually supplies the system layer
- AI factories and HPC programs increasingly buy integrated system platforms, not just generic racks plus components

## What to compare

- whether the company is delivering an actual compute system or only managed capacity
- degree of platform integration across accelerators, chassis, interconnect assumptions, and deployment packaging
- suitability for enterprise AI, hyperscale AI, or institutional HPC use cases
- readiness for liquid cooling, high rack power, and dense cluster deployment
- whether the vendor is an OEM, integrated platform supplier, or composable compute specialist

## Typical buyer questions

- Who is actually supplying the compute system, not only the facility or hosting layer?
- Is this an OEM server platform, a supercomputing platform, or a composable/integrated cluster system?
- How tightly does the platform couple to liquid cooling, rack power, and interconnect choices?
- Does this vendor belong in a system shortlist or only in the surrounding infrastructure shortlist?

## CTA

Use this category when the decision is about which compute platform belongs in the infrastructure stack, not only where the workloads will run.
